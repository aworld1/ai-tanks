Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) poses unique chal- lenges when agents must not only learn effective individual poli- cies but also coordinate with teammates to achieve collective goals. This complexity intensifies when roles and capabilities vary signifi- cantly across agents. In this work, we investigate the performance of two prominent reinforcement learning algorithms—Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Multi-Agent Posthumous Credit Assignment (MA-POCA)—in a team-based environment featuring heterogeneous agents. Each team comprises both short-range and long-range combatants, introducing diverse tactical considerations. The environment further incorporates a shrinking storm mechanic to ensure engagement and prevent stalling.
While PPO is often favored for its stability and simplicity, it is not specifically designed for cooperative credit assignment. MA-POCA, in contrast, aims to improve team outcomes by assigning credit even post-elimination of certain agents, potentially fostering more coordinated strategies. We train both algorithms under identical conditions for approximately 550,000 steps, using a single controlled environment seed to reduce variance. Contrary to our initial expec- tations, PPO produces more stable and simplistic behaviors within the limited training time, whereas MA-POCA’s theoretically supe- rior cooperative mechanisms do not clearly outshine PPO under these constraints.
Our results highlight the difficulty of achieving meaningful co- ordination and role complementarity in MARL within limited com- putational and training resources. They also underscore potential pitfalls in evaluation metrics, as we found Unity’s default ELO rat- ings to be skewed by persistent negative rewards from the damaging storm, complicating performance evaluation. This study contradicts the claims of superior performance made by the author’s of MA- POCA, and explains the lack of adoption within industry despite its claims of superior performance.
